Contact numbering (checked at 6x)
---------------------------------
- J102 (crop 7270 2560 7500 2710): the protocol convention holds. Each notch is
  immediately LEFT of its printed number. The box has an empty cell at its left
  end (7275-7298). Notches: 1=7300 2=7328 3=7355 4=7382 5=7409 6=7436 7=7463.
- J105 (crop 7680 2560 7910 2710): THE CONVENTION IS MIRRORED. Each notch is
  immediately RIGHT of its printed number. The empty cell is at the right end
  (7880-7905), and there is no notch to the left of "1" (the box edge is at 7693).
  Notches: 1=7719 2=7744 3=7770 4=7794 5=7822 6=7849 7=7876. These match the
  protocol's approximate J105 positions. The left-of-number reading cannot be
  right here: it would make the wired notch at 7876 a pin "8". Downstream
  passes should know that J105 is drawn mirrored.

K102, as drawn (context for J102-2/3/4/5)
- Coil: upper terminal is on 24V (J102-4) and lower terminal is on J102-3. D120
  is across the coil with its cathode on 24V, i.e. a flyback diode. A dashed
  linkage runs from the coil to the contact.
- Contact: one single-pole contact, drawn OPEN. The blade pivots on the LEFT
  terminal (~7165,1112). The left terminal is wired to the node formed by the
  C106 2.2/50V right-hand terminal, the R118 100k top and the L102 1.2mH top
  (dot at 7123,1112; C106 is fed from the op-amp output through R117 1k). The
  RIGHT terminal (~7212,1112) is a fixed contact going to R164, J102-2 and
  J102-5. So the line output reaches J102-2 and J102-5 only through the K102
  contact. The open-drawn contact suggests normally-open, inferred from the
  symbol alone.
- POWER MUTE hypothesis: compatible with the drawing. The card gives J102-3 as
  the coil low side (D120 anode) and J102-4 as the 24V coil supply. The drawing
  does not name either line.
- OUT SELECT hypothesis: nothing printed on the card identifies an "OUT
  SELECT" line among J102-1..7. The two contacts that are not relay-related
  (J102-1 and J102-6) both go to the heavy bus.
