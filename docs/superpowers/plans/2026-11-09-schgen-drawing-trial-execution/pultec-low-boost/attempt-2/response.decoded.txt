**1. Overall goal**  
The user wants a KiCad schematic that implements the net list that was provided.  
The net list defines 13 nets (GND, hi‑boost‑out, in, lo‑boost‑in, out, j10_p1…j10_p6, si_hi_boost_j20_p3, si_hi_cut_j12_p3, si_hi_cut_j4_p2).  
Each net is a set of pins that must be electrically connected.  
The schematic must contain the 17 symbols listed in the **PARTS** section, all placed with enough spacing on an A4 sheet (210 × 297 mm) and then all the wires must be drawn between the required pins.  
Finally, global labels must be added for the four “main” nets (hi‑boost‑out, in, lo‑boost‑in, out) and the corresponding pins must be connected to those labels.

**2. Choosing a convenient reference point**  
To keep the layout tidy we pick a central reference point for the **J1 connector** (the first “J1” symbol in the *connector* list).  
The connector is 5‑pin, so its pins run vertically.  We place it roughly at the centre of the sheet:  
`center_x = 150 mm`, `center_y = 150 mm`.  
All other symbols are positioned relative to this point with integer‑mm offsets so the schematic stays centred and the components do not overlap.

**3. Placement of each symbol**

| Symbol | Library | Value | Position offset from J1 | Rotation | Mirror | Reasoning |
|--------|---------|-------|------------------------|----------|--------|-----------|
| J1 | Connector_Generic | Conn_01x05 | (‑40, +20) | 180° | None | Place left of J1, rotated so pins are horizontal for easier routing. |
| J2 | Connector_Generic | Conn_01x05 | (+0, ‑30) | 0° | None | Bottom‑right of J1, same orientation as J1. |

**4. Adding the global labels**  
For each of the four main nets we create a label at the first pin of a “representative” component:

* **hi‑boost‑out** – connect to pin 2 of C1 (the “high‑side” side of the capacitor).  
* **in** – connect to pin 1 of J1 (the first pin of the J1 connector).  
* **lo‑boost‑in** – connect to pin 3 of J1 (the third pin of the J1 connector).  
* **out** – connect to pin 4 of J1 (the fourth pin of the J1 connector).

The label is placed a few millimetres away from the pin, to the side that keeps the wire short and the label readable.  The orientation (“left”, “bottom”, etc.) is chosen so the text does not overlap other symbols.

**5. Connecting all pins according to the net list**  

The net list is a direct map of which pins belong together.  For each net we form a single wire path that starts on the first component and ends on the last.  
All intermediate components are simply “joined” along the path.  
Because we use the `connect_pins` helper, we do not need to manually split wires; the helper will create a continuous net.

Example:  

* **GND** – all negative terminals of all capacitors (C1.1 … C6.1), the GND pins of J1 and J2, the ground pins of the rotary switch, and the second terminal of SI8 (the 100 kΩ resistor).  
  We connect each of these to the global GND symbol `#PWR_GND` that we place at the bottom‑left of the sheet.

* **hi‑boost‑out** – connects J1‑2, SI2.2, SI5.1, SI7.1.  
  We connect a wire from the first pin (J1‑2) to each of the other pins in the same net.

* **lo‑boost‑in** – connects J1‑3, R1.2, RV1.2, RV1.3, SI3.1, SI6.2, SW1.7.  
  A single net starts at J1‑3 and branches to each of the other pins.

* **out** – connects J1‑4, R1.1, SI7.2.  
  A wire from J1‑4 to the other two pins.

* **j10_p1 … j10_p6** – each of these nets is a separate two‑pin net:  
  * j10_p1: C1.2 ↔ SW1.1  
  * j10_p2: C2.2 ↔ SW1.2  
  * …  
  * j10_p6: C6.2 ↔ SW1.6  

  These are simple 2‑wire connections.

* **si_hi_boost_j20_p3** – connects SI1.2 to SI2.1.  
* **si_hi_cut_j12_p3** – connects SI3.2 to SI4.2.  
* **si_hi_cut_j4_p2** – connects SI4.1 to SI5.2, SI6.1, and SW1.7.  
  For this net we create a star‑shaped connection: a common node is placed on the central pin (e.g., SW1.7) and the other pins are connected to it.

**6. Wire drawing logic**  
Because all net connections are simple, most wires can be drawn as straight lines.  
Only the two “branch” nets (lo‑boost‑in and si_hi_cut_j4_p2) require a small “tee” or “cross” – but since the helper `connect_pins` can handle multiple connections to the same node, we simply call `connect_pins` for each pair; the API will merge them into one net.

**7. Mirror and rotation decisions**  
All symbols are kept at their default orientation except the two connectors which are rotated 180° so that their pin numbers increase from left to right (matching the usual connector layout).  
The rotary switch is left unmirrored; its pins are already oriented to the left side, making the connections to the 6‑position pins natural.  
No symbol is mirrored because the default layout already gives the shortest routing paths.

**8. Final step** – `write_out_all_wires()`  
After all `connect_pins` calls, we call `write_out_all_wires()` to serialize the wire data into the KiCad schematic file.
