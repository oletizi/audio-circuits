<|channel|>analysis<|message|>**1.  What the user wants**

The user has supplied a list of parts, nets and a few label names.  
The goal is to create a Ki‑Cad schematic that  
* places every component in a readable layout,  
* adds the four global labels (`hi_boost_out`, `in`, `lo_boost_in`, `out`),  
* connects the pins that belong to the same net, and  
* finally writes all the wires.

The Python wrapper functions provided already do the heavy‑lifting, so the script will simply call them in a logical order.

---

**2.  High‑level layout strategy**

* **Reference point** – The centre of the schematic will be at (150 mm, 110 mm), roughly the middle of an A4 sheet, so all other symbols are placed relative to this point.  
* **Rows** –  
  * **J1** (five‑pin connector) is the “main data bus” of the circuit.  
  * **J2** is a second connector that only provides ground pins.  
  * **R1** (56 kΩ resistor) sits to the left of J1 and is the “high‑boost‑in” path.  
  * **RV1** (47 kΩ potentiometer) links the two “joints” of the voltage‑divider.  
  * **SW1** (6‑position rotary switch) is placed on the right side of J1 so that each of its positions can be assigned to a separate net.  
  * **C1‑C6** and **SI1‑SI8** are arranged in a block underneath the switch; the capacitors are grouped in a row, the SI resistors in a column.  
* **Ground** – All ground pins (GND) will be connected to a single global GND symbol placed near the bottom‑left corner.

All offsets are chosen in multiples of ~10 mm so that the symbols do not overlap and wires can be drawn straight‑horizontal/vertical.

---

**4.  Adding the four global labels**

The user explicitly requested the following label names – they should be placed near the pin that belongs to the corresponding net and then wired to that pin.

| Label | Target pin | Near‑symbol | Offset from pin | orientation |
|-------|------------|-------------|----------------|------------|
| `in` | J1‑Pin_1 | J1 | +15 mm on the right | “right” |
| `lo_boost_in` | J1‑Pin_3 | J1 | +15 mm on the right | “right” |
| `hi_boost_out` | J1‑Pin_4 | J1 | +15 mm on the right | “right” |
| `out` | J1‑Pin_4 | J1 | +15 mm on the right | “right” |

The same pin (J1‑Pin_4) is used for both `hi_boost_out` and `out` because the net list shows that `out` is the same as `J1.4`.  The label text is added once and then connected to the pin with `connect_pins`.

---

**5.  Wire connections – net‑by‑net**

* **GND net** – all ground terminals (C1‑C6, J1‑Pin_5, J2‑Pins, SI8‑Pin_2, RV1‑Pin_1) are connected to the global GND symbol.  
  ```python
  connect_pins("#PWR_J2", "1", "J2", "Pin_5")
  connect_pins("#PWR", "1", "J2", "Pin_2")
  connect_pins("#PWR", "1", "J2", "Pin_3")
  connect_pins("#PWR", "1", "J2", "Pin_4")
  connect_pins("#PWR", "1", "J2", "Pin_5")
  for c in ["C1","C2","C3","C4","C5","C6"]: 
      connect_pins(c, "1", "#PWR", "1")
  for si in ["SI8","SI7"]:  # SI8‑2 and SI7‑2 are ground pins
      connect_pins(si, "2", "#PWR", "1")
  connect_pins("RV1", "1", "#PWR_J2", "1")
  ```

* **High‑boost‑in** – `lo_boost_in` net includes J1‑Pin_3, R1‑Pin_2, RV1‑Pin_2, RV1‑Pin_3, SI3‑Pin_1, SI6‑Pin_2, SW1‑Pin_7.  
  ```python
  connect_pins("J1", "Pin_3", "R1", "2")
  connect_pins("R1", "2", "RV1", "2")
  connect_pins("RV1", "2", "RV1", "3")
  connect_pins("RV1", "3", "SI3", "1")
  connect_pins("SV3", "1", "SI6", "2")
  connect_pins("SI6", "2", "SW1", "7")
  ```

* **Boost‑out** – `hi_boost_out` net consists of J1‑Pin_4, J1‑Pin_2, J1‑Pin_4, SI2‑Pin_2, SI5‑Pin_1, and SI7‑Pin_1.  
  ```python
  connect_pins("J1", "Pin_4", "J1", "Pin_2")     # J1‑4 & J1‑2 are a short
  connect_pins("J1", "Pin_4", "SI2", "2")
  connect_pins("SI2", "2", "SI5", "1")
  connect_pins("SI5", "1", "SI7", "1")
  ```

* **Out** – Same as `hi_boost_out` because the net list shows `out` = J1.4.  
  ```python
  connect_pins("J1", "Pin_4", "SW1", "6")    # SW1‑6 is the output of the switch
  ```

* **Switch‑to‑Capacitor nets** – each SW1 pin 1‑6 is connected to the 2‑pin of a capacitor:  
  ```python
  connect_pins("SW1", "1", "C1", "2")
  connect_pins("SW1", "2", "C2", "2")
  connect_pins("SW1", "3", "C3", "2")
  connect_pins("SW1", "4", "C4", "2")
  connect_pins("SW1", "5", "C5", "2")
  connect_pins("SW1", "6", "C6", "2")
  ```

* **Potentiometer‑to‑switch nets** – the internal switch pins of the potentiometer (MP and 1) are tied to the first two positions of the rotary switch:  
  ```python
  connect_pins("RV1", "MP", "SW1", "1")
  connect_pins("RV1", "1", "SW1", "2")
  ```

* **Resistor network** – the various resistor pairs that form the voltage divider or biasing paths are wired straight:  
  ```python
  connect_pins("R1", "1", "SI1", "1")      # R1‑1 to SI1‑1
  connect_pins("SI4", "2", "SI5", "1")      # SI4‑2 to SI5‑1
  ```

The order of `connect_pins` calls is not critical, but grouping by net makes the script easier to read.

---

**6.  Final steps**

After all `connect_pins` statements are issued, the helper function `write_out_all_wires()` is called once.  This instructs the KiCad API to render the netlist as physical wires on the sheet.

---

**Summary of the script flow**

1. **Imports & project path** – standard boiler‑plate that loads the KiCad schematic interface.  
2. **Define a central coordinate** – `center_x, center_y = 150.0, 110.0`.  
3. **Add every symbol** – using `add_schematic_symbol` with the calculated absolute positions, the chosen rotation (usually 0°) and no mirroring.  
4. **Add the four global labels** – each positioned a short distance to the right of the appropriate J1 pin, then connect them to that pin.  
5. **Connect all pins** – a series of `connect_pins` calls that follow the net list (GND, lo_boost_in, hi_boost_out, out, j10_p1‑p6, etc.).  
6. **Write all wires** – single call to `write_out_all_wires()`.
