# H7 platform phase 2: models, and the drawings they exist for

**Status:** design, awaiting the owner's approval.
**Follows:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-10-digital-pedal-platform-port-design.md`
(the port, phase 1, complete on `feature/digital-pedal-platform`).
**Inputs:** `docs/h7-platform/` and `circuits/h7-platform/module-interface.json`.

## Why this phase exists

The owner, 2026-11-10:

> *"I want to be able to review schematics generated by our canonical
> discipline as soon as is feasible. That will be the first asset I will
> actually be able to review as a human."*

> *"I can't make any informed review decisions until I can see schematics. I
> can't read your internal circuit encoding—which is fine. But I will have
> nothing to critique until schematics are available."*

The port produced documents and data. The models this phase writes are
TypeScript. Neither is something the owner reviews. **The first artifact he can
judge is a page drawn by `TOOLING.md`'s loop and shown to him under
`EVALUATING-DRAWINGS.md`.** So this phase is measured by one thing: how soon,
and how completely, the three boards reach him as drawn pages.

The port spec numbered modelling (phase 2) and drawing (phase 3) separately.
From the owner's side they are one piece of work, because the first is invisible
to him without the second. This design covers both, and sequences them so that
drawn pages reach him board by board rather than all models first.

## What follows from that

1. **The owner is asked no circuit question without a drawing.** Where the
   ported documents leave something open, the controller rules on it, labels it
   a controller ruling, and records it in the page's `RECORD.md` and in
   `docs/h7-platform/decisions.md`. The ruled part or value is on the drawn
   page itself, where the owner can see it and overrule it. The message that
   sends a drawing still carries a question and nothing else
   (`EVALUATING-DRAWINGS.md` §4). A summary of the rulings a page rests on, and
   of any practice change it needed, goes to the owner in a separate message,
   after his verdict or when he asks (controller ruling). He said so directly when asked whether he wanted any board changed: a
   question about the circuit, asked before he can see it, is one he cannot
   answer.
2. **The models are a faithful transcription of the pedals design as
   `docs/h7-platform/` records it.** No redesign. Any change the owner wants
   comes from reviewing the pages.
3. **The drawing practice is settled and is used as it stands.** The owner,
   2026-11-10: *"There has been an ENORMOUS effort in this repo to establish a
   canonical practice for generating schematics. We will NOT be relitigating ANY
   of that (unless we find a need to improve it)."* These boards do find such a
   need, in the specific places listed under **Changes to the practice** below.
   Nothing outside that list changes.
4. **Order of work is the controller's call.** The owner, 2026-11-10: *"I don't
   care what order you execute in."* The sequence below is the controller's
   ruling, chosen to put a drawn page in front of him soonest.
5. **Reviewing the models is agents' and tests' work, not the owner's.** The
   criteria a model is checked against come from the contract, the ported
   documents and the tests named here, never from the agent writing the model
   (CLAUDE.md, "An agent may never author the acceptance criteria").

## Goals

- **G1.** Each of the three boards (the processor module `h7-module`, the audio
  pedal board `h7-pedal`, the bring-up carrier) exists as a `Network` model in
  `circuits/h7-platform/`, transcribed from `docs/h7-platform/`, with every
  component, value and connection traceable to a cited line there (CLAUDE.md,
  "Transcription is evidence, not memory").
- **G2.** Each board is drawn by `TOOLING.md`'s loop as one or more pages,
  committed under `drafted/h7-platform/<board>/` with its `RECORD.md` and
  `repair/`, and every page is shown to the owner with nothing but a question
  (`EVALUATING-DRAWINGS.md`).
- **G3.** A drawn page reaches the owner as early as the work allows, and the
  boards then follow one at a time. The first page is not held back for later
  boards.
- **G4.** Every board's connections to the 40-pin module interface are checked
  by machine against `circuits/h7-platform/module-interface.json`. This is open
  item CONTRACT-MODEL-CHECK, and the contract is the acceptance criterion: it
  was written before any of these models.
- **G5.** Every controller ruling made to get a board drawn is recorded where
  the owner reviews the page that shows it (the page's `RECORD.md`), and in
  `docs/h7-platform/decisions.md`, labelled "Controller ruling (the owner may
  override)".

## Non-goals

- PCB layout, footprints chosen for fabrication, and the fully specified BOM.
  These are phase 4. The drawing path never reads a footprint
  (`tools/draught/intent-from-network.ts`), so a model may leave footprints
  unset.
- Simulation of the digital parts (the MCU, memories, logic, codec). The
  analog stages may be simulated where an existing model allows, but nothing
  in this phase depends on it.
- Firmware, the clock tree restatement (MODULE-CLOCK-TREE-RESTATE), and any
  open item this design classifies as layout, fabrication or bring-up work.
- Changes to the drawing practice beyond the list below.

## Anti-goals: what would make this a failure even if it technically works

- **A question to the owner about the circuit that is not attached to a page
  showing it.**
- **A placeholder part.** CLAUDE.md forbids fallbacks and mock data. Where a
  part is unchosen, the controller rules one, labelled; or the block is left
  undrawn and the page says so. Never a generic stand-in presented as a choice.
- **A drawing withheld for any reason but an electrical fault**, or described
  with *better*, *best*, *good*, *clean*, *readable* or *legible*
  (`EVALUATING-DRAWINGS.md`).
- **A controller ruling that reads as an owner decision**, or a superseded value
  drawn as current. The port's labelling rules carry over unchanged
  (`docs/h7-platform/README.md`).
- **A check that compares the model with itself.** The first netlist a new board
  is checked against cannot be one the same model produced without saying so;
  see **Changes to the practice**, C2.
- **Drawing practice changed quietly.** Each change is listed here, recorded in
  `TOOLING.md` where it lands, and limited to what these boards need.
- **The pedals v1 schematic, or any file the old generator made, used as an
  authority** (port spec, "No generated artifacts from the old tooling").

## Sequence (controller ruling)

The ruling: **one board at a time, each one carried all the way to a drawn
page in front of the owner before the next is modelled, smallest first.** Each
step below ends with pages shown to the owner.

1. **The carrier, without its loopback.** About 25 parts, so it fits the loop
   as it stands today: one page, the size of the largest pages already drawn.
   It also exercises everything a larger board needs except multiple pages: a
   board that is not a perfboard (C1), a first netlist for a board that has
   never had a schematic (C2), the module interface modelled from the contract
   and checked against it (G4), and symbols chosen under C4. Faults in that
   plumbing then surface on a small page the owner can see.
2. **Multiple pages per board (C3)**, built and shown on the module.
3. **The module.** This needs the MCU pin map derived first (see **The MCU pin
   map**) and controller rulings on the VDDA bank, the ESD part and the ID
   strap.
4. **The pedal**, block by block. Its pages are shown to the owner as each
   block is drawn, not held until the whole board is done.
5. **The carrier's loopback**, after a controller ruling on its form. If the
   ruling reuses the pedal's codec and SAI isolation, they come in through
   `include()` from the pedal's blocks, not as copies.

**The contract freeze runs alongside, not ahead.** Pedals owner decision O-10
put the freeze before the carrier. The owner's direction of 2026-11-10
(`docs/known-gaps.md` G311, recorded as an amendment on O-10 in
`decisions.md`) puts drawn pages first and hands the order to the controller.
So pages are drawn against the draft `module-interface.json`, and the freeze
proceeds in this phase: FB-AN2606, DATASHEET-REVISIONS-RECHECK,
MIDI-IN-RECEIVER-STOCK, STACK-HEIGHT-OWNER-REPORT, then CONTRACT-FREEZE. When
the freeze changes the contract, G4 re-runs on every board and every page it
changes is redrawn and sent again. STACK-HEIGHT-OWNER-REPORT is a report, not a
circuit question: it goes to the owner in a message of its own, never with a
drawing.

**A board is finished for sequencing when its pages have been sent to the
owner, not when he has given a verdict** (controller ruling). He gives verdicts
on his own schedule, and the work does not stall waiting for one. When a verdict
asks for a repair or a change, that work comes before further work on the next
board, because the owner's review is the scarce thing and his feedback should
not queue behind new pages.

The owner may reorder this at any point. The builder may also change it if a
step turns out blocked, provided the reason is recorded in the plan's execution
record and pages still reach the owner board by board.

## Changes to the practice

These are the places where the loop as it stands cannot take these boards. The
reconnaissance found each one in the code, cited below. Each change is limited
to what the boards need, is recorded in `TOOLING.md` (or the skill) where it
lands, and is reported to the owner in the separate message that follows the
first page depending on it (see point 1 under **What follows from that**).

- **C1. A board that is not a perfboard.** A board is discovered by its
  `boards/<board>/perfboard.json`. That declaration requires a `vrt` field
  (`tools/perfboard/declaration.ts:121-153`), and `make check` requires the
  VeroRoute layout to exist (`tools/perfboard/check.ts:283-286`). None of these
  boards is a perfboard. **Criterion:** each h7 board is discoverable by
  `make draw` and joins `make check` without a `.vrt`, and the checks it runs
  are the ones that apply to it: model validation, the contract check (G4),
  and the drawn-page round trip. **Not allowed:** a placeholder `vrt` path, a
  stub layout, or a skipped check that looks like a passing one. The mechanism
  is the builder's choice: a second declaration kind, or a field that marks a
  board's fabrication class.
- **C2. The first netlist of a board that has never had a schematic.**
  `make draw` compares the intent's net partition against a committed netlist
  fixture (`tools/cli/draw.ts`, `tools/draught/draw-draft.ts:189-201`). That
  fixture has always been `kicad-cli`'s export of an existing schematic. These
  boards have none. **Criterion:** the check that binds a drawn page is that
  `kicad-cli`'s export of the page agrees with the model. That check exists
  already as the round-trip tests, which list their boards by hand
  (`tests/circuits/drafted-kicad-round-trip.test.ts`). Any fixture written from
  the model before a page exists is labelled as model-derived in its file and in
  the page's `RECORD.md`, so nobody reads the pre-draft comparison as an
  independent one. The h7 pages join the round-trip tests.
- **C3. More than one page per board.** The loop drafts exactly one sheet per
  board and re-papers it to US Letter (`tools/draught/draw-render.ts:105-125`).
  The largest page drawn so far holds 44 symbols. The 34-part staged preamp
  overflowed Letter by 110 mm before repair. The module and pedal are several
  times that. **Controller ruling on the shape:** a board is drawn as **one page
  per functional block**, each block being one `include()`d sub-network of the
  board's model. Each page goes through the loop exactly as a one-page board does
  today. This follows the Pultec pattern of one page per section and keeps
  component atomicity (CLAUDE.md, "Composition"). **Criteria:** every component
  of the board appears on exactly one page; a net that crosses pages carries the
  same name on every page it appears on; and the union of the pages' exported
  netlists agrees with the board's model. A block too large for a Letter page is
  split into smaller blocks; the page is never shrunk. Whether the pages are also
  assembled now as one hierarchical KiCad project is the builder's choice, but
  phase 4 will need one netlist per board, so nothing may rule that out.
- **C4. Symbols the installed KiCad libraries lack.** Copperhead resolves
  symbols from the installed KiCad libraries. No custom-symbol path has been
  shown to work. A check of KiCad 10.0.5's installed libraries
  (controller, 2026-11-10) found exact symbols for STM32H743IITx, PCM3060,
  W25Q128JVS, TPS62933F, G6K-2, USBLC6-2SC6, SN74LVC2G14DBV, TPS3808DBV,
  LM66100DCK, MCP6001, AO3400A, AO3401A, 2N7002, PMEG4030ER and 1N4148W, and
  generic `Device` and `Connector` symbols (crystal, ferrite bead, fuse, test
  point, USB-C receptacle, 1x20 and 2x05 headers). It found none for W9825G6KH,
  TPS7A49, TPS7A24, TLV803, TS3USB221, OPA1688, SN74LVC1G34, SN74LVC3G34,
  SN74LVC1G07, AOD4185, BZT52C12, PESD12VL1BA, ISOM8710, the NX3225 crystal or
  the X1G004 oscillator; PMEG4005 has variants but not the AEA. The search
  matched symbol names by prefix only, so an alias under another name may
  exist. **Rule:** use the part's own stock
  symbol where it exists. Otherwise use a stock symbol whose pins match the part
  pin for pin, verified against the part's datasheet and recorded per part with
  that citation, while the model's `part.mpn` names the real part. Add a
  project symbol only where neither exists, and only if the builder first shows
  copperhead can draw one. Footprint and symbol names are verified against the
  libraries on disk, never written from memory (CLAUDE.md).
- **C5. The toolchain in this worktree.** Copperhead and akcli are not
  installed here. `draughting/install.sh` installs them, at the versions pinned
  in `draughting/tools.lock.json`. This is setup, not a change to the practice.

Anything else the boards turn out to need from the practice is raised the same
way: stated, cited, limited, and shown to the owner with the page that needed
it.

## The MCU pin map

The module cannot be drawn without the STM32H743's internal pin assignments.
The port deliberately did not carry them as fact (`processor-and-memory.md`,
MODULE-INTERNAL-PIN-MAP). This phase derives them:

- **Sources:** ST's pin data `STM32H743IITx.xml`, read from the pedals
  repository at the pinned commit
  (`git -C /Users/orion/work/pedals-work/digital-pedal-platform show 5b926d0:<path>`,
  path per `docs/h7-platform/README.md`), whose upstream is
  STMicroelectronics/STM32_open_pin_data at `7d1f151`, BSD-3-Clause. Also the
  datasheet DS12110. The pedals records (CORE-BRIEF §6, the split spec's §6.2)
  are cited as the starting assignment and checked, not trusted.
- **Scope:** about 50 signals (FMC SDRAM about 37, QUADSPI 6, HSE 2, SWD, the
  ID strap ADC pin), plus every supply pin. The 31 interface pins and the fixed
  ones (SDNWE PH5, SDNE0 PH3, SDCKE0 PH2, PA11/PA12, PB14/PB15) are already
  given.
- **Criterion:** every assignment names a pin function that ST's data lists for
  that pin, and no pin or net is used twice, across the module's internal
  signals and the 31 interface ports together. A test checks both, so the
  "never ran" collision check of the pedals record runs here.
- If the pin data is committed into this repository, it goes in with its
  licence and provenance, beside the board that uses it. Whether to commit it
  is the builder's choice.

## Open items: which ones this phase settles

The 29 rows tagged "phase 2" in `docs/h7-platform/open-items.md` fall into
three groups. The grouping is the reconnaissance's reading, adopted here as a
controller ruling.

- **Must be settled before the board's page is correct**, by a controller
  ruling shown on the page:
  - **Module:** MODULE-INTERNAL-PIN-MAP; VDDA-WIDEBAND, 3V3D-RIPPLE-INTO-VREF-A
    and VDDA-DAMPING-LEG, treated together. The present bank fails R-57, and the
    ruling must meet the stated f0 ≤ 158 kHz with its derivation written out.
    Also the ESD part in USB-DFU-VERTICAL.
  - **Carrier:** CARRIER-LOOPBACK-FORM.
  - **Pedal:** MIDI-IN-RECEIVER-STOCK, for the MIDI IN block only. Until a
    stocked part is ruled, that block is left undrawn and the page says so.
- **The modelling work itself, or a ruling that lets drawing proceed:** the
  rows INTERFACE-, MODULE-POWER-, PEDAL-POWER-, PEDAL-ANALOG- and
  BYPASS-PARTS-TO-MODEL; VBUS-HOTPLUG; VREF-A-BLEEDER-DECISION; TEST-PAD-SPLIT
  (with its `### Conflict:` entry); CONTRACT-MODEL-CHECK (G4);
  MODULE-3V3-BUDGET; MODULE-BUCK-LOOP-STARTUP; SWD-PINOUT-FORMAL-SPEC.
- **The contract freeze, run alongside the drawing** (see **Sequence**):
  FB-AN2606, DATASHEET-REVISIONS-RECHECK, STACK-HEIGHT-OWNER-REPORT and
  CONTRACT-FREEZE, with MIDI-IN-RECEIVER-STOCK above.
- **Left for layout, fabrication or bring-up**, whatever their tag:
  MLCC-DC-BIAS, MODULE-CLOCK-TREE-RESTATE, MODULE-PART-HEIGHT-READINGS, MODULE-HEADER-SOURCING,
  FOOTSWITCH-PAPER-READINGS, USB2-SPEC-UNREAD. Each one's row gets its phase
  corrected in `open-items.md`, with the reason.

A row this phase settles is closed in `open-items.md` by citing what settled it
(a ruling in `decisions.md`, a test, or a committed page), never by paraphrase.
That rule carries over from the port.

## Left to the builder

- Which model kind each part uses. There is no regulator, relay, crystal, LED,
  FET, fuse or ferrite kind; `ic`, `switch` and `connector` have open pin
  vocabularies (`lib/model/kinds.ts`). Adding a kind is allowed if a part needs
  one, with a test beside it ("Refuse rather than guess").
- How the module interface is modelled: the contract already splits it into
  `J_MOD_A` and `J_MOD_B`, and `PartSpec.pinField` exists for one pin field
  split across components.
- The block boundaries within each board, subject to C3's criteria.
- The mechanisms for C1 and C2.
- File layout under `circuits/h7-platform/`, by the rule in CLAUDE.md: sources
  there, generated files under `generated/`, prose in `docs/h7-platform/`.
- Group assignment on a page, net kinds, and the repair, all by `TOOLING.md`.

## Risks the owner should know about

- **Repair cost may grow badly with page size.** The staged preamp's 34 parts
  took a 380-operation repair. Nothing here has measured pages above 44
  symbols. C3 caps page size by splitting blocks, which is the main protection.
- **Digital buses have no naming rule in the practice** (`TOOLING.md`, "Net
  naming has no rule"). The FMC and SAI blocks will put dozens of named nets on
  a page. If that turns out to need a rule, it is raised under the
  practice-change process above, with the page that showed it.
- **The carrier is the least specified board.** Most of its parts will be
  controller rulings. That is why it goes first: it is small, and the owner
  sees those rulings on one page.
